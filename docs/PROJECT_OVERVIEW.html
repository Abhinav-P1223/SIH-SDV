01 The problem

Why an Indian road defeats software that was written for a lane.

SIH26037 asks for adaptive path planning and collision avoidance on unstructured Indian roads. That one word carries the whole difficulty. Almost all publicly available autonomous-driving software assumes a structured road: painted lanes, traffic that stays inside them, and road users whose next move follows from a small set of rules. An Indian road frequently offers none of that.

No lane markings

Village and rural roads have no centreline to detect. The primary input of a lane-follower simply does not exist.

Mixed traffic

Motorcycles, auto-rickshaws, bicycles, cars, buses and trucks share one carriageway. "One vehicle per lane" is meaningless.

Pedestrians & cattle

They enter the carriageway without warning. The threat is not in a lane and never was.

Sudden crossings

A cow, a child, a cyclist emerging from between parked vehicles. Demands reacting to a predicted path, not a current position.

Informal merging

Vehicles join from side roads without yielding or signalling. Right-of-way cannot be inferred from road geometry.

Narrow roads

Two vehicles cannot pass. Sometimes the only correct action is to reverse — a manoeuvre most stacks cannot perform.

The core failure

A lane-following stack answers one question: "where is the centre of my lane, and how do I stay on it?" On an unstructured road that question has no answer. Worse, when lane detection fails there is nothing to fall back on, because every downstream module was written in terms of a lane. The failure is not graceful.

02 Our core idea

Stop looking for a lane. Look for space.

Traditional
"Find the lane centreline, then follow it."

Requires paint. Degrades to nothing when the paint is absent.

Ours
"Find the safest useful path through the drivable corridor."

Requires only road edges and a direction of travel. Paint is optional metadata nothing reads.

At every planning cycle the system:

  1. Understands the available drivable corridor — two road edges plus a reference direction toward the goal.
  2. Predicts where every nearby road user will be over the next few seconds.
  3. Evaluates collision risk against those predictions.
  4. Generates many candidate trajectories inside the corridor.
  5. Discards the infeasible and the colliding, then scores and selects the best.
  6. Hands the trajectory to a controller that drives a vehicle model.
It is a loop, not a pipeline

The vehicle's own motion changes what the sensors see next cycle, which changes the tracks, the predictions, the risk, and therefore the plan. The plan is re-derived from scratch 10 times per second; the controller and vehicle dynamics run at 50 Hz. Nothing is scripted — no scenario contains a pre-authored ego manoeuvre. Every behaviour you observe emerges from these modules interacting.

03 Complete system flow

Thirteen stages, one closed loop, fifty times a second.

World / scenario

A YAML file defines the road corridor, the ego's start and goal, and the other road users with their behaviours. Advances every 20 ms; the only source of truth.

Sensors

Sensor models, not renderers. Each converts ground-truth agents into noisy measurements subject to FOV, range, occlusion, dropout, rate and latency.

Perception

Emits Detection records already in the world frame: position, a 2×2 covariance, plus whatever that sensor knows — class, extent, radial speed.

Sensor fusion

Combines all three sensors into one estimate per object. Kalman filter on [x, y, vx, vy]; radar radial speed enters through an EKF update.

Tracking

Persistent identities across frames. Tentative on first sight, confirmed after 3 hits, deleted after 1.0 s without an update.

Prediction

Projects each track 4 s ahead with a constant-acceleration model, growing positional uncertainty with time.

Risk / TTC

Sweeps the ego's predicted footprint against every object's, producing TTC, a collision probability and a level from NONE to CRITICAL.

Behaviour FSM

Converts risk into a policy: target speed, whether lateral avoidance is allowed, whether only stopping is acceptable, whether reversing is permitted.

Adaptive planner

Generates candidates inside the corridor under that policy, and selects one. Never returns nothing.

Collision checking

Rejects candidates that leave the corridor, exceed steering limits, or breach the safety margin against a predicted footprint.

Controller

Stanley for steering, PID for speed. Turns the chosen trajectory into steering angle, acceleration and brake.

Safety supervisor

Ignores the plan's reasoning entirely. Looks only at physical TTC, risk level, and whether the planner failed. Can override with full braking.

Vehicle dynamics

Kinematic bicycle model with actuator saturation. The only code permitted to change the ego pose.

05 Perception

Three sensors, because each answers a different question well and the others badly.

Camera — what is it?

20 Hz · 60 m · 100° FOV
Position with range-dominated covariance, object class, class confidence.

Weak at: range — monocular depth error grows with distance.

LiDAR — where, how big?

10 Hz · 80 m · 360° FOV
Position with tight isotropic covariance, plus object length, width and heading.

Weak at: no class, no direct velocity.

Radar — how fast closing?

20 Hz · 120 m · 60° FOV
Position with bearing-dominated covariance, and a direct radial speed measurement.

Weak at: coarse bearing; small objects may be missed.

Why three, not one

Their errors are complementary, not merely redundant. The camera's covariance is long and thin along the viewing ray; the radar's is thin across it. Fusing them gives a position better than either alone. And radar's radial speed is a direct measurement — it converges an object's velocity estimate far faster than differentiating positions ever could.

Two learned camera components

Both built and validated offline on real Indian-road imagery:

Drivable-space segmentation

Fast-SCNN trained on IDD-Lite turns a road photograph into a drivable-area mask, from which a corridor is extracted. That corridor can drive the real planner through a safety gate — the planner cannot tell it apart from a corridor loaded from YAML. Drivable IoU 0.877.

Object detection

Faster R-CNN MobileNetV3-320-FPN fine-tuned on UVH-26, detecting Indian traffic participants including auto-rickshaws — a class COCO does not have. Full results in section 16.

06 Sensor fusion

One estimate per object, with honest uncertainty attached.

The idea in three lines

Camera tells us what an object probably is.
LiDAR tells us where it is and how big.
Radar tells us how fast it is closing on us.

Kalman filter

Each track carries state [x, y, vx, vy] and a 4×4 covariance. Between measurements the state is predicted forward on a constant-velocity model with acceleration process noise; on arrival it is corrected, weighted by the relative confidence of prediction and measurement.

Radar EKF

Radial speed is not linear in the state — it is the object's velocity projected onto the sensor-to-object direction, minus the ego's own. That is linearised about the current estimate, making it an extended Kalman update. The only non-linear update in the filter.

Covariance

Every published track carries a real 2×2 positional covariance, not a placeholder. Prediction grows it with time and the planner uses it to prefer routes clear of uncertain objects — so an uncertain object is treated as more dangerous, correctly.

Mahalanobis gating

Before associating a detection with a track we ask whether it is plausibly this track given its uncertainty — a distance normalised by covariance, gated at χ² = 9.21 (99%, 2 dof). Anything outside every gate starts a new track rather than corrupting an existing one.

Timestamp-aware measurements. Camera, LiDAR and radar do not fire simultaneously and each has its own latency. Each track therefore carries an explicit state_time — the moment its state is valid at, which is not the same as the time of its last measurement. Measurements too stale, too far out of order, or stamped in the future are dropped rather than silently applied.

Shipped disabled — deliberately

Full temporal fusion (predicting each measurement to its own timestamp) is implemented and tested, but ships off. Enabling it makes the vehicle measurably more conservative, because it removes an over-confidence the existing collision margins were tuned against. Turning it on is a planner-retuning decision, deliberately out of scope. See docs/PHASE4_TEMPORAL_FUSION.md.

07 Tracking

A detection is a moment. A track is a story — and prediction needs the story.

Why one detection per frame is not enough. A detection is a single instant with no identity. It cannot tell you velocity — that needs two observations of the same object. It cannot survive a moment of occlusion. And a sensor that drops out for one frame would, without tracking, look exactly like an object that ceased to exist.

08 Motion prediction

Four seconds ahead, with an uncertainty envelope that grows honestly.

Constant-acceleration model

The predictor is the only module that sees successive ObjectStates, so it estimates each object's acceleration itself — least-squares slope over recent velocity history, low-pass filtered, clamped per class. With too little history the acceleration is zero and it reduces exactly to constant velocity. Beyond a short window the speed is held: constant acceleration is not credible for long.

Intent priors

For free movers — a pedestrian, a cow — the sign of acceleration is read as intent. Braking hard? Probably about to stop, so grow uncertainty less. Speeding up? Committing to the crossing, so grow it more. A small, explicit, tunable prior.

Road-following prior

A vehicle whose class follows roads and whose heading is within 30° of the corridor is predicted along the corridor, not in a straight line, with its small lateral velocity decaying — that component is mostly estimation noise.

Uncertainty growth

Positional uncertainty grows with the square of time, seeded by the track's own covariance and scaled per class. A pedestrian's envelope balloons faster than a bus's, which is correct.

Why this beats reacting to current position

At 10 m/s a vehicle covers 10 m in the second it takes to notice, decide and brake. A system that only sees where a cow is commits to a plan that is already wrong. Seeing where the cow will be, with an uncertainty envelope, lets the planner steer around it early and gently rather than braking hard and late.

Not a neural predictor

This is an explicit kinematic model with hand-set priors. Deliberately so: it is inspectable, it degrades predictably, and every one of its assumptions is written down.

09 Risk and TTC

Distance ranks threats backwards. Something better is required.

Naive
distance ÷ closing speed

A cow 5 m away at the roadside scores worse than a cow 30 m away walking into your path. It also ignores direction entirely.

Ours
swept footprint overlap under uncertainty

The ego's rectangle is swept against each object's predicted rectangle over 4 s, and weighted by probability, time and object class.

Three views of the ego's own motion

ViewQuestionConsumed by
Route"If I proceed along my route at desired speed, what happens?"Behaviour FSM — so a threat stays visible after the planner has already slowed
Physical"At my current heading and speed, what happens?"Safety supervisor. A stopped vehicle has infinite physical TTC
Planned"Along the trajectory I actually chose, what happens?"Behaviour FSM — is the current plan clear?

From the distance series d(t) the engine derives: whether it ever falls inside the safety margin; the TTC — the first time it does while still closing; a collision probability p(t) from d(t) and prediction uncertainty σ(t); and a risk score w_type × maxt[ p(t)·exp(−t/τ) ] with τ = 2.0 s, so near-term threats dominate and a pedestrian outweighs a truck.

LevelTriggerEffect
NONEscore < 0.05Cruise
LOWscore ≥ 0.05Cruise or follow
MEDIUMscore ≥ 0.20→ CAUTION, speed capped to 60%
HIGHscore ≥ 0.45 or route TTC < 3.0 s→ AVOID, lateral avoidance prioritised
CRITICALphysical TTC < 1.2 s→ EMERGENCY_BRAKE
A deliberate ceiling

The risk-score ladder tops out at HIGH. CRITICAL can only be raised by the physical view. That is why a stopped vehicle facing an obstacle is HIGH, not an emergency — it is not moving, so it is not about to hit anything.

10 Behaviour / decision

Seven states, fourteen transitions, all in one readable table.

Two hysteresis mechanisms

Escalation to a more severe state is immediate — safety must not be delayed. De-escalation requires a minimum dwell of 0.3 s and must clear a lower exit threshold than the entry threshold (caution_exit_score 0.10 against entry 0.20; avoid_exit_score 0.25 against entry 0.45). Together these stop the machine chattering.

11 Adaptive path planning

The heart of the project — and the reason it works without lane markings.

The corridor frame

The road is a drivable corridor: a left boundary, a right boundary, and a reference polyline giving the direction of travel toward the goal. The reference is not a lane line — it is a direction. Width may vary along the road and need not be symmetric.

Positions are expressed in corridor (Frenet) coordinates: s = distance along the reference, d = lateral offset (+ left). Planning in (s, d) rather than (x, y) is exactly what makes the approach lane-independent — "move 1.5 m right of my current line" is one number, on a straight road or a curve.

The planning cycle

How a candidate is built

Lateral — quintic polynomial

In arc length σ, matching the ego's current offset, heading and curvature at σ = 0, reaching d_end with zero slope and zero curvature at σ = S. Quintic because six boundary conditions need six coefficients; matching curvature at both ends is what makes the path steerable without a jerk at the join. S is chosen so peak curvature stays inside the steering limit — so a wide shift from a crawl is generated feasibly rather than generated too sharp and thrown away.

Longitudinal — jerk-limited S-curve

Seeded from the ego's current acceleration, so re-planning at 10 Hz does not repeatedly restart from zero acceleration and pin the vehicle near zero for ever. A hard stop keeps the instantaneous full-braking ramp: safety beats comfort.

Feasibility filter — hard rejections

ReasonTest
CURVATURE_LIMIT|κ| > tan(δ_max)/L — the wheel does not turn that far
LATERAL_ACCELERATION_LIMITv²|κ| > a_lat_max — comfort and grip
OUTSIDE_DRIVABLE_SPACEAny footprint corner leaves the corridor at any time
PREDICTED_COLLISIONFootprint enters the safety margin of a predicted object
TERMINAL_STATE_EXPOSEDContinuing from the end pose gets hit beyond the horizon

That last rejection matters more than it sounds. It rules out both "stop exactly where you will be run into" and "creep toward a route that is already blocked" — the two ways a myopic planner makes a locally reasonable, globally fatal choice.

Scoring — 13 weighted components

collision

Soft risk, probability-weighted and time-discounted. Non-zero even for candidates that pass the hard check — so the planner prefers routes clear of uncertain predictions.

clearance · boundary

Distance to the nearest predicted object footprint, and to the road edge.

progress

How far along the corridor it gets. Its weight is multiplied the longer the ego stands still, so a feasible bypass eventually beats freezing.

smoothness · curvature · jerk

Ride quality and steering effort.

consistency

Deviation from the previously selected offset. With noisy perception the cheapest side flips every cycle; committing beats dithering.

front_pass · blocked

Penalties for cutting in front of a crossing object, and for a continuation that meets an object beyond the horizon.

The planner never returns nothing

Cheapest feasible candidate that makes progress wins. If nothing is fully feasible there is a graded fallback: collision-free candidates violating only the boundary margin are selected and flagged degraded. Only if none exist does it return a hard-stop flagged fallback — which the behaviour layer and safety supervisor both treat as an emergency.

12 Collision checking

The vehicle is a rectangle, not a point — and that changes everything.

Our ego is 4.20 m long and 1.80 m wide, on a 2.60 m wheelbase. A trajectory whose centreline clears an obstacle by 0.5 m may still put a corner through it, especially while turning: the footprint sweeps a wider band than the path itself.

Oriented-box footprints

Ego and every predicted object are rotated rectangles at all 41 time steps of the horizon.

Separating-axis distance

True minimum distance between two oriented rectangles — not a centre-to-centre approximation.

Swept in time

Ego footprint at time t against each object's predicted footprint at the same t. Comparing against current positions would miss every moving threat.

Clearance, not just a boolean

The checker returns the minimum distance achieved, which feeds the scorer — so among several safe candidates the planner prefers the one that keeps more room.

Two refinements stop the planner freezing for bad reasons. Within a short grace window the current pose is not a violation — the ego's present position was not the planner's choice. Beyond that, sitting inside the margin counts as a hit only while the gap is still shrinking: sliding past a parked truck at the clearance you already have is allowed. Actual overlap never is.

Geometrically valid ≠ behaviourally desirable

The checker only answers "does this hit anything?" A candidate that swerves violently into the oncoming side, clears everything by 2 cm and ends facing a blocked route is geometrically valid and behaviourally terrible. That is what the scorer is for. Keeping the two separate means safety is a hard constraint and quality is a soft preference — they never trade against each other.

13 Controller and vehicle model

Planner proposes. Physics disposes.

Stanley lateral control

δ = heading_gain · θ_e + atan( k · e / (k_soft + v) ) + atan(L · κ_path)
  • θ_e — heading error against the path at a look-ahead point
  • e — cross-track error of the front axle
  • The third term is a curvature feed-forward: the steering the path's curvature requires anyway, so feedback only has to correct error rather than generate the whole turn

PID longitudinal control

u = a_ff + kp·e + ki·∫e + kd·de/dt        e = v_ref − v
acceleration = max(u, 0)      brake = max(−u, 0)

With trajectory acceleration as feed-forward and anti-windup — the integral is clamped and is not accumulated while the output is already saturated in the direction of the error.

Actuator limits and vehicle model

Actuator saturation

δ_target = clip(δ_cmd, ±δ_max)
δ[k+1] = δ[k] + clip(Δ, ±rate·dt)
a_net  = clip(a − b, −a_brk, +a_acc)

Rate limit 0.785 rad/s. The planner never gets instantaneous steering.

Kinematic bicycle

β = atan( lr/L · tan δ )
ẋ = v·cos(ψ+β)   ẏ = v·sin(ψ+β)
ψ̇ = v·cos β·tan δ / L   v̇ = a_net

Integrated at 50 Hz. Speed is signed — negative is reverse — so reversing falls out of the same equations.

Planned path ≠ actual vehicle motion

The vehicle cannot turn instantly, cannot brake instantly, and its steering has a rate limit. If we teleported the ego along the planned trajectory, every result would be meaningless — we would be measuring the planner's imagination. Because the vehicle lags the plan, and the next plan starts from where the vehicle actually is, the loop is closed and the numbers mean something. Measured worst-case tracking error across all 22 runs: 0.303 m.

14 Safety supervisor

A simple guard that does not trust the complicated part.

The planner is the most complex component in the system. If its cost weights are wrong, if the corridor is wrong, or if it simply finds nothing, nothing downstream of the planner is in a position to notice — the controller faithfully follows whatever it is handed.

The supervisor runs every dynamics step, after the controller and before the vehicle model, and deliberately does not read the planner's reasoning. Its TTC threshold (1.0 s) differs from the risk engine's CRITICAL threshold (1.2 s) on purpose: they are independent checks with independent thresholds. Every activation is recorded with its time and reason.

15 Reverse recovery

Sometimes the only way forward is backwards.

On a narrow Indian road a vehicle can end up with a parked car ahead, a road edge on one side and an obstacle on the other. A forward-only planner has exactly one legal action there: stop and wait forever. Reversing a few metres to gain the room a lateral shift needs is the real solution — and it is a manoeuvre human drivers on such roads perform constantly.

The planner side

After 3.0 s STOPPED with no feasible forward candidate, the FSM enters REVERSING and permits reverse candidates. The planner generates curved reverse trajectories using the same quintic machinery as forward planning, evaluated against distance travelled backwards. It commits to a leg and re-plans within it.

The controller side

The reverse branch originally steered on heading error alone — and on a straight reverse path that error is already zero, so the wheel never turned. It now also takes a cross-track term measured at the rear axle (the end that leads while reversing), clamped hard, because reversing is non-minimum-phase and an unbounded command will fishtail.

Limitation

Reverse is a single committed leg. The vehicle cannot yet shuffle back and forth in a multi-point turn.

UVH-26 is CCTV, not dashcam

It is elevated fixed-camera imagery, and the held-out test split is CCTV too. So CCTV-to-dashcam transfer remains entirely unvalidated. These numbers say the model learned Indian traffic participants from a fixed elevated camera. They do not say it will work from a vehicle windscreen.

Two validations that must not be blurred

The detector was evaluated independently on held-out imagery. It does not consume rendered camera pixels in the Phase 8 closed-loop simulator — the simulator renders no image frames, so there are no pixels for it to consume. "Sensors mode" in the closed-loop runs means the simulated camera/LiDAR/radar models feeding the same Detection contract into the same tracker. This project does not claim end-to-end learned camera perception in closed loop.

What was verified is the output contract: the detector's Detection objects were fed to the unmodified tracker and shown to be consumed correctly. That is an integration proof, not a closed-loop performance claim.

17 Datasets

Three datasets, each answering exactly one question it is able to answer.

IDD-Lite 42 MB

Drivable-space segmentation. The only openly available Indian-road semantic dataset with a drivable/non-drivable split at a size trainable on CPU.

nuScenes mini 5.1 GB

Multimodal fusion validation. The only accessible dataset carrying synchronised camera + LiDAR + radar with range-rate, real calibration and real timestamps.

UVH-26 subset 2.5 GB

Indian-scene detection fine-tuning. Human-verified consensus labels (not machine pseudo-labels), CC BY 4.0, and it contains auto-rickshaws.

Why not one giant dataset

No single available dataset answers all three questions. nuScenes has real multimodal sensor timing but is Boston and Singapore — no auto-rickshaws, and only 911 distinct physical objects, far too few to train a detector. IDD-Lite has Indian roads but its instance labels are unusable for detection (they repeat the semantic ids). UVH-26 has Indian traffic participants with trustworthy labels but is fixed CCTV, so it cannot validate a dashcam pipeline and carries no LiDAR or radar. Each was chosen for one question, and we state the limits of each rather than over-claiming from any.

None of them is needed to run the project

The simulator, every scenario and the final validation need no datasets at all. Both trained checkpoints ship inside the repository. All dataset files are gitignored and must never be committed. Download links and setup steps: docs/SETUP_GUIDE.md.

On the 189.2 ms outlier

One cycle out of 5,601 exceeded the 100 ms budget. The p95 figure — the number the budget is actually written against — is 55.0 ms, comfortably inside. We report the outlier rather than only the percentile.

Why the emergency brakes occurred

All 7 are in sensors mode: dense market 3, reverse recovery 3, pedestrian dart 1. They are absent from ground-truth mode, which identifies the cause as perception noise, not a planning defect — a briefly noisy track can produce a momentarily critical physical TTC.

A correction worth stating

It would be neat to say all 7 fired because zero trajectories were feasible, but the recorded evidence does not support that. The captured pedestrian-dart activation shows the trigger was physical TTC 1.00 s at 9.2 m/s, with 1 of 2 candidates still feasible. The supervisor has three independent triggers and different events fired different ones.

Emergency braking is correct behaviour, not a defect

20 What makes our approach different

Engineering decisions, stated without exaggeration.

1 · Lane-independent corridor planning

lane_markings exists in the road model as an informational field that nothing reads. Remove it and the system is unaffected — which is the entire point of the problem statement.

2 · Prediction before planning

Every candidate is checked against where objects will be, not where they are.

3 · Explicit collision geometry

Oriented rectangles and separating-axis distance, swept over the whole horizon — not point-to-point distance.

4 · Continuous re-planning from scratch

10 Hz from the ego's actual pose, with no committed plan to go stale. 5,601 cycles across validation.

5 · Independent safety override

The supervisor does not read the planner's reasoning. If the most complex component is wrong, something simpler still catches it.

6 · Fusion with honest covariance

Real 2×2 covariances flow through prediction into planning, so an uncertain object is treated as more dangerous rather than rounded to a point.

7 · Reverse recovery

Curved reverse trajectories with a matching rear-axle controller term. Proven necessary by ablation, not assumed.

8 · Closed-loop vehicle dynamics

Actuator saturation and a bicycle model between plan and motion, so the numbers describe a vehicle rather than a planner's imagination.

9 · Indian-scene perception adaptation

A detector fine-tuned on human-verified Indian imagery that can see auto-rickshaws — with its limitations measured and published rather than omitted.

22 One-page summary

If you read only this section, you have the whole project.

Problem

Indian roads have no reliable lane markings, mixed traffic of wildly different sizes and speeds, pedestrians and cattle entering without warning, informal merging, and lanes too narrow to pass. Lane-following autonomy has no valid input here, and fails ungracefully when its one input disappears.

Our idea

Do not look for a lane. Understand the drivable corridor, predict what everything nearby will do, evaluate collision risk against those predictions, generate many candidate trajectories inside the corridor, and continuously select the safest one that makes progress.

Architecture
World → Sensors → Perception → Fusion → Tracking → Prediction → Risk/TTC
      → Behaviour FSM → Adaptive Planner → Collision Check
      → Controller → Safety Supervisor → Vehicle Dynamics → World ↺

A closed loop: 10 Hz planning, 50 Hz control.

Key technologies

Kalman filter with EKF radar update and Mahalanobis gating · constant-acceleration prediction with intent priors and uncertainty growth · corridor-frame (Frenet) lattice planning with quintic lateral profiles and jerk-limited speed profiles · oriented-box separating-axis collision checking · 7-state behaviour FSM with dual hysteresis · Stanley + PID with actuator saturation · kinematic bicycle model with signed speed for reverse · independent safety supervisor · Fast-SCNN drivable-space segmentation · Faster R-CNN MobileNetV3 detector fine-tuned on Indian imagery.

Validation

11 scenarios × 2 perception modes = 22 runs, each verified at runtime for correct wiring and no ground-truth leakage. Learned perception validated separately and offline on real Indian imagery.
